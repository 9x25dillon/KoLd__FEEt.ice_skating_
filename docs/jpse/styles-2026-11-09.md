# Career styles, measured: 9 November 2026

`npm run jpse:styles` from `tools/ice-lab`. Each style is in `game/styles.ts`, and
each one is nothing but authored multipliers on the physics params (L3, untuned).
This page records what the physics made of them, on the Experimental setup at full
mastery. The jump columns come from JPSE's first grid (225 loop entrances). The rest
come from three fixed runs:

- **Stroking:** two-foot pushes from standstill for 20 s.
- **Hard carve:** a full lean on one foot at 6 m/s for 3 s.
- **Serpentine:** a 0.7 lean, side to side every 0.6 s, eight times, at 5 m/s.

Heights and spins are means over the entrances that left the ice.

## Read

- **Power** does what you described. It jumps 16% higher, carries 32% more spin and
  skates fastest. It pays with its edges: fewer entrances leave the ice, it falls on
  the hard carve, it falls first in the serpentine, and none of this grid's
  entrances (tuned for the neutral skater) lands for it. A power skater needs its
  own entrances. JPSE can search for them.
- **Finesse** holds the whole serpentine (the transitions) and pays in speed,
  height and spin, as described.
- **Technician** holds the serpentine **and** still lands the double loop. On these
  measures it does everything Finesse does, and more. Finesse's dance side
  (balance, the free leg's authority) isn't measured by any of these runs yet. It
  needs its own measure, a spiral or free-leg hold, or a tune that separates the
  two. That's the next decision.
- **Endurance** keeps 20% more legs through the stroking run and jumps lower.

## Career

You choose a style on the career board. Its cost is paid from that moment, and half
its benefit comes with it. Each week of style drills adds a quarter of mastery,
toward the full benefit. A style you put down keeps its mastery but loses a quarter
at every season step. Weeks of drills win it back. No style is the default, and saves
from before styles load with none.
