Edgework · Pre-production Plan

The Feel Prototype

Is carving fun with no jumps, no score and no art?

Pre-production is not a small version of the game. It exists to answer one question that the entire project rests on, and to answer it cheaply enough that a no is survivable. No shipped game has used analog lean plus analog knee pressure as its primary verb. If the answer is no, no amount of costume rendering, judging fidelity or career structure rescues it. This plan delivers that answer in week 16 for about $468,000, rather than in month 22 for eight million.

01

Scope discipline

Most pre-productions fail by building a small version of everything. This one builds the feedback channels and nothing else — because the question is about how the verb feels, and feel is what the player perceives, not what the simulation computes.

We build

The carve solver — the thing under test.

Balance, stumble, fall — failure is half of feel. A verb you cannot fail is not being tested.

Blade tracings — one week of work, and the primary visual channel for edge quality.

Edge tone audio — the bible calls this the primary audible channel for edge quality. Testing feel in silence tests the wrong thing. Three days for a crude version.

Procedural lean — a capsule that stacks over its edge feels different from one that tips. Feel engineering, not art.

The Edge Ribbon — the only HUD element, and it is under test too.

Telemetry — because the gate is decided on data.

We refuse to build

Jumps as elements · rotation · scoring · judges · any named element · music · characters (a capsule with a blade) · costumes · cloth · hair · an arena (a plane, a boards ring, a grid) · menus · AI · netcode · career · the Composer · mocap.

Every one of these has an advocate who will make a reasonable case during week 6. The answer is no. The list exists so that saying no costs nothing.

The hop is not a jump

From week 9 the prototype gets a load-release vertical hop with no rotation, no element identity and no scoring. The load/release trigger shares its input channel with carving, so it has to be tested — but it arrives after carving has been evaluated alone, so that an exciting jump cannot rescue a boring foundation. If carving only works because jumping is exciting, the shipped game is 90% tedious connective tissue.

One system pulls double duty and should be built properly rather than as a throwaway: the telemetry recorder is the replay recorder from the bible's netcode section — a deterministic input stream plus periodic state keyframes. In pre-production it is the measuring instrument. In production it is ghost opponents, server-side verification and the Composer. Built once.

03

Three control schemes

The project's primary risk requires two fallbacks prototyped, not one. These deliberately span the space rather than clustering around variations of the same idea — one is the bible's proposal, one is safer and conventional, one is stranger with a higher ceiling. All three sit behind a runtime toggle and are labelled A / B / C in every test, never "the real one."

Scheme A · the hypothesis
Lean & Load

Left stick is a lean vector — direction and magnitude, 0–65°. RT is knee pressure. Right stick is carriage. LT is the free leg and toe pick.

Highest ceiling, highest risk. Three simultaneous continuous analog channels is more than most players carry. This is what is actually under test.

Scheme B · the safe fallback
Steer & Load

Left stick becomes intended travel direction, as in any third-person game. The solver picks whichever edge curves that way; lean magnitude is derived from turn demand and speed. RT unchanged.

Keeps the carve physics, the emergent radius and the load-release takeoff. Loses deliberate inside/outside edge choice — the sport's actual alphabet. If B beats A the game is still viable, just less expressive, and three sections of the bible need rewriting.

Scheme C · the strange one
Two-Foot

Left stick controls the left blade's edge, right stick the right blade's. Maps directly onto crossovers, mohawks and choctaws, which are inherently two-foot actions.

Possibly unlearnable. Possibly the most distinctive control scheme in any sports game. Worth four weeks of an engineer to find out, because if it works the game becomes much harder to copy.

Down-select

Three to two at week 8, two to one at week 12. Only one scheme enters the gate build — but the runner-up stays compiled and playable, so that an AMBER outcome has somewhere to go on day one rather than after a month of rebuilding.

04

Week by week

Weeks are relative to project start. Calendar anchoring is the producer's.

Month 1 · The blade on ice

W1

Repo, CI, build farm. Skating consultant starts. UE5 validation spike — a carve on a plane, an ice material, a tracing render target. One engine, not two: the engine decision closed ahead of week 1.

W2

M1.1 — The ice renders. Planar reflection cost measured, tracing render target writing at simulation rate, and a custom skate movement component scaffolded in place of Unreal’s built-in character movement.

W3

Solver v0: one foot, lean → carve, longitudinal friction. Capsule with a blade on an infinite plane. Debug camera. No balance, no second foot.

W4

M1.2 — You can carve a circle, and its radius changes with lean. Internal only; nobody outside the room sees this. First telemetry logging per tick.

Month 2 · Three schemes, one question

W5

Solver v1: two feet, edge switching, stroking, crossovers, the skid threshold. Ice grid with damage and snow.

W6

Tracings v0 — contact points accumulating into a render target that drives the ice material. Edge tone audio v0 from the contracted designer.

W7

All three control schemes implemented behind a runtime toggle. Procedural lean layer online.

W8

M2 — Internal Feel Review. Eight team members plus eight friendly externals, blind A/B/C, ranked. Soft gate: down-select 3 → 2. A scheme selection, not a kill gate.

Month 3 · Depth and the hop

W9

Balance model, stumble, fall, powered-ragdoll v0. The load-release hop — no rotation, no element identity, no score.

W10

The Figure Eight and Slalom courses built as instrumented, repeatable tasks. Telemetry pipeline complete and dashboarded.

W11

Both surviving schemes refined against internal telemetry. Edge Ribbon v1. Tuning pass on the bite curves with the consultant.

W12

M3 — Learning curve demonstrated internally, then down-select 2 → 1 and freeze the gate build. No code changes after this point except crash fixes. Gate thresholds signed (§08).

Month 4 · The gate

W13

Protocol pilot with four testers. The purpose is to debug the protocol, not the game — script timing, task legibility, telemetry capture, facilitator consistency. Pilot data is discarded.

W14–15

20 testers × 2 sessions. Ten testers per week, sessions three to seven days apart.

W16

THE KILL GATE. Analysis, written report, binding decision, and a postmortem regardless of outcome.

06

Telemetry

Captured per tick: input state, full skater state, edge sign, lean, skid flag, contact point. Roughly 4 KB/s, so a full session is a few megabytes.

MetricDefinitionWhat it tells you
Free-play durationSeconds of voluntary play after "stop whenever you like"The headline. Nothing else is close.
Figure-eight deviationRMS distance of the tracing from the reference curve, in metresThe skill metric. Repeatable, objective, and it is also the shipping tutorial.
Skid ratioFraction of ticks where the edge let goFalls as skill rises. A flat line means no learning is happening.
Mean lean depthMean absolute lean while movingRises with confidence. The best single proxy for "they trust the physics."
Edge changes / minuteSigned edge transitionsRises with vocabulary. Distinguishes exploring from surviving.
Time-to-retrySeconds from fall to next inputUnder three seconds means the failure felt fair.
Unprompted actionsBehaviours nobody asked for, coded from videoThe mastery signal.

The playtest protocol

Recruitment

Twenty external testers, screened into four segments so that a wrong-audience result is visible rather than hidden inside an average.

SegmentnProfile
Sim-curious8Plays Trials, Skate, soulslikes. The primary audience.
General gamers6No particular affinity. The breadth check.
Skating fans4Watches or skates. Any gaming level.
Skaters who barely game2The authenticity check. Their verbatims matter more than their scores.
Excluded: game developers, anyone who knows the team, anyone who has heard the pitch. Both sessions are paid up front, so that returning is not a financial decision and attendance is never mistaken for enthusiasm.

Exit questions

Only five, forced-choice where possible, because Likert scales invite politeness.

  1. "When you fell, was that you or the game?" — attribution. A good hard game makes players blame themselves.
  2. "What were you trying to do that you could not?" — reveals whether the ceiling reads as depth or as obstruction.
  3. "Describe the feeling of a good turn to someone who has not played it." — if they can, the verb is legible. Verbatims here are the best marketing copy you will ever get.
  4. "Would you rather play this again next week, or [named comparable game]?" — forced choice.
  5. "Is there anything you want to ask us?" — unprompted requests for a build are logged, never solicited.

Facilitator rules

  • Never say the word "fun."
  • Never explain a failure during a measured block. Not once.
  • Never defend the prototype.
  • Log every unprompted utterance verbatim, including swearing — sworn delight and sworn frustration are the two most informative data points you will collect.
  • Two observers minimum: one on video and verbatims, one on telemetry.
  • Schemes are labelled A/B/C to the testers and to the observers.
08

The kill gate

Signed in week 12

These thresholds are agreed and signed by the decision-maker before any external data exists. Writing thresholds after seeing results is how kill gates become theatre.

Must pass — all three

1
Voluntary free play, session 2

Median ≥ 8 minutes of the 20 available

Eight minutes of self-directed play with no goals, no score, no art and no remaining novelty is a strong signal that the verb carries itself.

2
Skill improves

Median figure-eight deviation improves ≥ 35% from attempt 1 to attempt 20

A verb with no measurable learning curve has no mastery ladder — and mastery is the entire product.

3
Failure feels fair

≥ 70% answer "me" to attribution, and median time-to-retry < 3 s

If falling feels like the game's fault, every hard element in the shipped game will feel unfair.

Signal — informative, not fatal

#CriterionTarget
4Unprompted experimentation in free play≥ 50% attempt something nobody asked for
5Unprompted requests for a build or release date≥ 6 of 20
6Ceiling not reachedThe strongest tester is still improving at the end of session 2
7Cross-segment consistencyNo segment's median free-play under 4 minutes

Decision rule

Green

all three must-pass met

Proceed to the vertical slice on the winning scheme. Week 17 starts. Confirm the mocap booking.

Amber

two of three met · or all three met on a fallback scheme but not on A

One six-week extension, single scheme, re-tested with 12 testers against the same thresholds. If A failed and B passed, the bible's control scheme, turn matrix and assist ladder are rewritten around B before the slice begins.

Red

one or fewer met

Stop. Do not proceed to the vertical slice. Write the postmortem, publish the data, and either pivot the concept or return the remaining money.

Two rules that make this real

  • The extension can be taken once. Its ~$150k is approved in week 12 as part of the gate sign-off, so that invoking it is a pre-authorised branch rather than a political act.
  • The decision-maker is not the person who invented the control scheme. The creative lead presents nothing and does not vote. The designer presents the data; the studio head or an external advisor decides. Sunk cost beats judgement every time it is allowed to.
09

How this gate could give a wrong answer

A gate you have not tried to break is a ritual. Four failure modes, each with a mitigation already built into the protocol above.

Failure modeMechanismMitigation
False positive — noveltyTracings are genuinely novel and pretty. Testers enjoy drawing on ice, not carving, and the team ships a game with one good screenshot.Session-2 free play is the headline metric precisely because novelty has decayed. Criterion 2 requires demonstrated skill growth, which novelty cannot fake.
False positive — politenessTesters are in a room with the people who made it and want to be kind.Forced-choice exit questions. Behavioural metrics outrank every stated preference. Facilitators never signal what a good answer looks like.
False negative — jankTesters reject an untextured capsule on a grey plane, and the team reads it as rejecting the verb.All three feedback channels ship. Exit question 2 separates "how it looked" from "how it responded," and any tester citing presentation is flagged in the analysis.
False negative — wrong twenty peopleThe sample simply is not the audience.Four screened segments, reported separately. Criterion 7 exists to make a segment split visible rather than averaged away.
What this gate deliberately does not test

Whether a four-minute judged program is compelling. That needs elements, scoring, music and stakes, and it belongs to the vertical slice's exit gate. Pre-production tests the verb. Do not let scope creep in under the argument that "we should check the whole loop" — that is a twelve-month prototype, and the entire point here is to fail cheaply.

11

Weekly cadence

WhenRitual
MondayBuild lock for the week. Whatever is in at 10:00 is what gets played.
WednesdayThe Session. All eight play the current build for 30 minutes in the same room, in silence. Then 30 minutes of discussion. The producer and the consultant play too — no exceptions, no observers.
ThursdayConsultant review: one authenticity note per week, escalated as a bug.
FridayTelemetry review against the internal learning-curve trend. Fifteen minutes, dashboard only, no slides.

The silence during play matters. The moment someone narrates what the build is supposed to do, you stop measuring the build and start measuring the explanation — which is the same reason the facilitator script exists in §07.

What green unlocks

Week 17 begins the vertical slice: one rink at ship quality, one skater with full costume, cloth and groom, one complete 2:40 short program containing every element category, the nine-judge panel, the Broadcast Director, the Wwise blade layer, and the Kiss & Cry. Twenty-two people, six months, and an exit gate of its own — a stranger plays for twenty minutes and asks when it ships.
